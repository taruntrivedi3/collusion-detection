# What this experiment can establish

## The exact guarantee for the oracle benchmark

Let A be an independent bank of honest games used to fix the edge and subset normalizations. Let M_A(D) be the maximum subset score for trajectory D with that fixed transformation.

If D0 is an honest observed game and D1,...,D999 are independent games from exactly the same honest simulator, then, conditional on A, these 1,000 trajectories are exchangeable. Their scalar scores are exchangeable too, even if the score's state representation is insufficient or its mutual-information estimates are biased. Therefore

```
p = (1 + count(M_A(Db) >= M_A(D0), b=1,...,999)) / 1000
```

is super-uniform. With no ties, its rejection probability at 0.05 is exactly 0.05; counting ties in the upper tail is conservative. State histories must evolve according to the same honest process in the reference and observed games. A wrong simulator invalidates this argument. Fitting a score from the observed game also requires a separate justification; this experiment avoids that dependence in the main comparison.

The independent empirical recalibration has a similar argument. Conditional on the normalization bank, the complete randomized procedure maps independent honest games and independent reference RNG streams to identically distributed raw p-values. Rank a new p-value among 1,000 independent honest calibration p-values, with small p-values considered more extreme. This is a valid rank comparison under that same honest environment. Ties are conservative; a method with highly discrete or saturated p-values can undershoot 5%. The result is marginal over the random calibration bank; a particular fixed bank can give a conditional size above or below 5%, which the independent test games estimate.

## Why endogenous history does not automatically cause false alarms

The main comparison fixes a normalization estimated from known honest games. That is an explicit source of honest-model knowledge for all three methods. In an omitted-state setting, this bank already contains honest unconditional dependence. Subtracting its mean and changing edge/subset scales can suppress apparent omitted-state failures, especially when other independent edges dominate the maximum. The source-style A/B sensitivity is needed to assess the original detector; near-5% results for a no-history row with oracle normalization do not establish that history is unnecessary.

A within-state shuffle generally does not preserve every possible full trajectory of an endogenous game. That observation by itself does not determine the distribution of the maximum MI statistic or the direction of its error.

There is a useful exact conditional-mean fact for the primary scripted policies. Let F_(t-1) contain all available history just before round t, and let S_t be the sufficient public state. Define

```
u_i,t(a) = 1{agent i chooses a at t} - pi_i(a | S_t)
```

For different honest agents i and j, conditional independence of their fresh action draws gives

```
E[u_i,t(a) * u_j,t(b) | F_(t-1)] = 0.
```

These centered pair residual products are martingale differences: they have zero conditional mean even when the state is generated by previous actions. Products from different times have zero covariance under the usual finite-moment conditions. This helps explain why action/state feedback need not inflate a correctly conditioned dependence statistic. It is not a finite-sample proof for the plug-in conditional-MI subset scan, which estimates marginal probabilities, handles sparse cells and maximizes over subsets. A full asymptotic result would need regularity, visitation and nuisance-estimation arguments. The reported Monte Carlo tests are evidence for the specified regimes, not that missing theorem.

## Why a circular shift can give a different answer

The implemented circular null shifts each agent's entire action sequence while leaving the recorded public state sequence in place. It preserves the cyclic ordering of the individual sequence. It does not preserve the agent's action distribution conditional on that public state.

For example, under a unique Rock leader and reaction probability 0.5, a leader-reactive agent plays Paper with probability 2/3 and the other two moves with probability 1/6 each. A within-state shuffle preserves that state's empirical proportions. A whole-column circular shift moves actions sampled under other public states into those rows. Consequently its reference joint tables, sparse-cell behavior and score distribution can change. Maintaining serial order alone does not establish calibration for a state-conditioned statistic. Our comparisons measure this effect; they do not claim that every circular-shift method is invalid. A different shift construction or statistic needs its own test.

## Claim suitable for the paper

Report the observed calibration and power with explicit policies, state definitions, denominators and uncertainty. If sufficient-state within-state tests remain near 5%, say so; do not frame hypothetical invalid histories as an observed failure. Separate the omitted-state failure from adaptation under sufficient state. The known-policy simulator supplies a finite-sample oracle benchmark and an empirical calibration tool. Unknown LLM policies, public/private history, action order, common news and policy changes are further modeling tasks. Statistical dependence and group membership errors remain separate from economic collusion and intent.
